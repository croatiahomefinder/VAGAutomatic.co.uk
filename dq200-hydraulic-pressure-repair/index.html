Real-world symptoms

Does your DQ200 behave like this?

Drivers often search the symptom before they ever see the fault code. These are the descriptions we commonly associate with a DQ200 hydraulic pressure problem when the diagnostic codes support it.

  • Hydraulic pump keeps running, runs very frequently or runs for an unusually long time
  • Gearbox warning appears and drive is lost or restricted
  • Car slips into neutral or loses drive while manoeuvring or driving
  • PRNDS or the selected gear / spanner symbol flashes on the dash
  • Vehicle may not select drive immediately and then works after waiting
  • Fault clears temporarily but returns when pressure demand rises
  • Visible mechatronic fluid leakage or evidence of pressure loss
Important

The symptom alone is not enough.

Judder, clutch slip, harsh shifts and gear-selection problems can have clutch or mechanical causes. We use the full scan, the pressure-related DTCs and the behaviour of the unit to decide whether the £399.99 hydraulic/mechatronic repair is the correct route.

Diagnostic wording

Fault codes and scanner descriptions we recognise.

ODIS, VCDS and generic diagnostic tools do not always display the same sentence. We match the DTC, any VAG number shown, the complete code set and the symptom.

P17BFHydraulic pump / overload or protection wording. Often seen when the pump has been running too long because pressure is not being built or retained as expected.
P189C / 006300Function restriction due to insufficient pressure build-up. This is one of the strongest DQ200 pressure-loss indicators when the symptoms match.
P1895Function restriction due to pressure drop. Different tools and software revisions may phrase the description differently.
P0841 / 17225 / 10784Transmission fluid pressure sensor / pressure signal plausibility wording. P0841 on its own is not enough to assume one failed component; we look at it in the context of actual pressure-loss symptoms and the other codes.

What is outside this service

A mechatronic pressure repair is not a gearbox rebuild.

  • Clutch pack wear or clutch mechanical failure
  • Internal gearbox / gearset damage
  • External wiring, battery, power or vehicle-side faults
  • Separate DQ200 TCM electronics / no-communication faults
  • Damage so severe that the original unit is no longer viable to repair

If testing shows the fault sits outside the £399.99 service, we tell you. If we cannot complete the supported repair, there is no repair charge.

FAQ

Questions people ask before sending a DQ200.

What does P189C mean on a DQ200?

P189C describes a restriction caused by insufficient hydraulic pressure build-up. On a DQ200 it is commonly seen with pressure-loss symptoms and may appear with P17BF or other hydraulic-related codes. We use the complete code set and symptoms rather than one code in isolation.

Does £399.99 cover the whole DQ200 hydraulic repair?

For a supported DQ200 hydraulic/mechatronic pressure fault such as P17BF, P189C or P1895, £399.99 covers the repairable internal mechatronic cause required to resolve that supported fault. We do not add component-by-component surcharges. Clutches, mechanical gearbox damage and separate TCM electronics faults are different services.

Why does the pump keep running?

If hydraulic pressure is not being built or retained correctly, the pump may run more often or for longer while the system tries to reach its target pressure. Persistent pump activity together with pressure-related fault codes is a useful diagnostic clue.

Can I send the unit from Europe?

Yes. We accept repair-and-return shipments from Europe. The customer pays carriage both ways and should use the courier's repair-and-return or temporary-export process where applicable.

What if the mechatronic is beyond repair?

Testing is free. If we cannot complete the supported repair, there is no repair charge. The customer remains responsible for courier costs in both directions.
