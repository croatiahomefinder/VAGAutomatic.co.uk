How people actually search

The warning on the dash is often more useful than the name of the part.

Many customers do not know whether the failed component is a TCM, mechatronic, pressure circuit or sensor system. They know what the car did. Our pages are written around both the diagnostic code and that real-world symptom.

“Only even gears are working”

Common wording around the P1735/P1736 fault family on DQ381 and DQ500 applications.

“Gearbox emergency mode”

Often accompanied by losing one gear set, intermittent recovery after restart, or a fault that appears once warm.

“DSG pump keeps running”

A common way drivers describe DQ200 hydraulic pressure-loss problems associated with P17BF, P189C and related codes.

“It works again after restart”

Intermittent transmission faults can disappear temporarily after an ignition cycle. The stored DTC is still important.

“PRNDS is flashing / car went into neutral”

Useful DQ200 symptom language when pressure is not being maintained or drive is lost.

“Garage says I need a mechatronic”

Before buying a complete replacement, check whether your exact fault is one of the repairable fixed-price failures we support.

Repair quality

The repair inside matters more than the casing outside.

We use quality-controlled, traceable repair components and automotive-grade materials. Where supported Bosch DQ381/DQ500 hardware requires component replacement, we use LuK / Schaeffler parts matched to the controller version.

We do not publish our internal repair method or supplier recipe. What the customer gets is the original unit repaired where viable, bench checked before return and backed by a 2-year repair warranty.
