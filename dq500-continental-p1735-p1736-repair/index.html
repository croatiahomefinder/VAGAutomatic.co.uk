Real-world symptoms

How owners and garages usually describe this fault.

The stored DTC matters, but the gear pattern and when the fault appears are often just as useful.

  • “Gearbox in emergency mode” or transmission warning on the dash
  • Only even gears available, or only odd gears available
  • One clutch path / half the gear set disappears
  • Reverse may remain available on some fault patterns while other gears are lost
  • Switching the ignition off and back on can temporarily restore normal operation
  • Fault may be intermittent and appear more often once the transmission is warm or after a longer drive
  • P1735 / P173500 or P1736 / P173600 stored in the transmission controller

ODIS, VCDS & generic scanners

Code aliases and descriptions.

Manufacturer-specific wording varies by tool database and software version. These are the forms we want customers to recognise.

P1735 / P173500 / VAG 10666Position sender for clutch 1, electrical fault. Generic tools may instead describe clutch 1 position sensor electrical malfunction or a manufacturer-specific clutch position fault.
P1736 / P173600 / VAG 10668Position sender for clutch 2, electrical fault. Generic tools may instead describe clutch 2 position sensor electrical malfunction.
Diagnostic tools: ODIS, VCDS, Autel, Launch, Topdon and generic OBD databases can show different wording for the same underlying manufacturer DTC. We use the code, VAG symptom number where available, controller hardware and fault behaviour together.

Testing

We confirm controller identity, electrical function and relevant sensor plausibility on the bench. A bench repair does not replace the final vehicle checks. Basic settings, adaptations and correct operation must be confirmed after the unit is refitted.

If the unit does not match the supported fault family, we do not force it into the fixed-price repair. Free testing still applies.

FAQ

Questions people ask before posting the controller.

How do I know whether my DQ500 is Bosch or Continental?

Do not decide from the gearbox name alone. DQ500 exists with different controller generations. Send a clear photo of the TCM/mechatronic label and the complete diagnostic scan so we can identify the correct route.

Do P1735 and P1736 qualify for the fixed price?

On a supported Continental DQ500 controller, P1735/P1736 pressure/position-sensing faults are the fixed-price repair family on this page. We still confirm the part number and fault details before you post the unit.

What about P0607, P0715, P0716, P0851 or gear-monitoring faults?

Those can be repairable on some Continental units, but they do not automatically qualify for the P1735/P1736 fixed-price repair. Send the complete scan first and we will tell you whether to send the controller.

Can the fault be intermittent?

Yes. Customers can see emergency mode, one gear set missing or temporary recovery after a restart. The stored codes and controller identification are important even if the car drives normally again later.

Do you accept European mail-in repairs?

Yes. Customers across Europe can send their own used controller for repair and return. The customer pays carriage both ways and should use the courier's repair-and-return or temporary-export process where applicable.
